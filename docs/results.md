# Measured results

All numbers are from runs in this repository's development container
(4 vCPU, Linux 6.18, no IPv6, loopback networking). Absolute values will
differ on real HoloPorts and phones; the comparisons between runs are the
point. No phone was used: the "device" is a desktop conductor process that
is stopped and started.

## Milestone 3: mobile lifecycle with stale peers, two relays and storage arcs

`scripts/mobile-lifecycle-e2e.sh`, repeated by `scripts/lifecycle-benchmarks.sh`.
Raw `results.json` of every run (including the device helper's report and
C's gossip attempts): [`results-data/lifecycle/`](results-data/lifecycle/).

Setup of every run:

* Local `kitsune2-bootstrap-srv` 0.5.3 (bootstrap + iroh relay),
  `GOSSIP=fast`, bootstrap **enabled** for every conductor including C.
* A: stock `holochain 0.7.1`. B and D: holochain 0.7.1 + `upstream/holochain-0.7.1`
  (dev profile), each with its **own** relay (`OpsIntegrated` source, 30 s
  reconciliation poll), own journal and own registration key. C: stock or
  patched per variant.
* 1. C syncs genesis and subscribes to both relays. 2. C stops. 3. A
  authors 34 notes (102-106 new DHT ops). 4. B and D integrate them; **A
  stops** (its record stays valid in the bootstrap service: a stale peer).
  5. C's client syncs hints from both relays (`--policy all`, deduplicated)
  and fetches both peer records. 6. C starts. 7-8. `hc-push-device` adds
  the records (C verifies the signatures), optionally requests a round, and
  waits until every required op is integrated, `Accepted` and
  `locally_validated` in C's own `DumpOpTimings`. 9. C stops.
* Times are from C's conductor process start. CPU is C's `utime+stime` from
  `/proc` up to that point (includes conductor start-up). Kitsune2 bytes
  are the sums over C's open connections (`DumpNetworkStats`) at that point.
  HTTP bytes are C's client counters for the hint sync and both peer
  requests.

Variants:

| Variant | C conductor | C config | Device helper |
|---|---|---|---|
| `baseline` | stock 0.7.1 | defaults (connect timeout 22 s) | adds peer records only |
| `rto8` | stock 0.7.1 | `network.request_timeout_s: 8` (connect timeout 3 s) | adds peer records only |
| `patched-noreq` | patched | defaults | adds peer records only (control: the patch alone changes nothing) |
| `round` | patched | defaults | adds peer records, then `RequestGossipRound` for B and D |
| `zero` | patched | `target_arc_factor: 0` | `--arc empty`, requests rounds; then reads 3 of A's notes with `get` |
| `zero-rto8` | patched | zero arc + `request_timeout_s: 8` | as `zero` |
| `round-relay-restart` | patched | defaults | as `round`; relay B is stopped while A authors the middle third of the notes and restarted before the last third |

### Reading the numbers

* **Stale peers.** With released Holochain and bootstrap on, catch-up
  depends on whether Kitsune2 happens to pick the stale peer first: 5 of 8
  baseline runs took 48–71 s, 3 took about 6 s. The patched conductor
  without a request behaves the same (2 of 2 slow), so the patch does not
  change ordinary scheduling.
* **`RequestGossipRound` removes the tail.** With the relays' peers
  requested first, 8 of 8 runs finished in 6.2–6.4 s, with A in the peer
  store and bootstrap enabled. C's gossip never initiated with A (no
  failed initiation in any `round` run). The requested peers
  came from two independent relays; the device chose them, and C verified
  their signed records before they could be requested.
* **What is left is start-up.** Conductor ready ~1.9 s, own agent rejoined
  and peers known ~2.0 s, first round ~5.1 s (waiting for the iroh home
  relay), all ops validated ~6.3 s. Fetching, validating and integrating
  ~102 ops took about 1.1 s. The ~3 s relay-registration wait is the next
  thing to remove (U3, item 5).
* **Shorter connect timeout (`rto8`)**: bounded at 10–13 s, but still
  spends 5–7 s on A, and shortens every network request of the conductor.
* **Notification vs data sync.** Detection (B/D integrated → relay journal)
  p50 9–14 ms. C's HTTP catch-up of all hints from both relays took
  33–47 ms. Data sync (conductor start → all validated) took 6.2–6.4 s with
  the request and up to 71 s without. Notification is not the bottleneck.
* **Bytes.** Hints for ~102 ops from two relays: ~89 KB in, 1.4 KB out
  (hint sync plus two peer-record requests of ~730 B each). Each relay
  served the full set and the client deduplicated D's copy (`--policy
  all`); syncing only until one relay succeeds (`--policy first`) would
  halve that. The hint sync was 6 requests (two capability checks, two
  pages per relay). Kitsune2 on C's open connections: ~69–75 KB received,
  ~17–20 KB sent for the same ops.
* **CPU.** C's conductor used 2.9–3.2 s of CPU until synced with the
  request, about 2 s of it start-up, against 2.5–2.6 s in fast and
  3.6–4.6 s in slow baseline runs.
* **Zero arc.** The relays skipped every located op hint (`arc=empty`):
  0 hints, 4.3 KB in. C held none of A's ops and needed no gossip catch-up.
  Reading three of A's notes with `get` worked, served by B/D, but the
  first `get` after waking was routed to stale A and failed after ~44 s
  (~6 s with `rto8`); the retry and later gets took ~0.2 s.
  `RequestGossipRound` does not help `get` (U3).
* **Missed notifications** (`round-relay-restart`): relay B was down while
  A authored the middle third. Its journal still ended up with all 102 ops:
  54–58 via `OpsIntegrated` (before the stop and after re-subscribing),
  44–48 reconciled by the `DumpOpTimings` poller from its checkpoint (hence
  the ~4.7 s maximum detection latency in that row). C's catch-up was
  unaffected (6.3–6.4 s).

### Limits of these measurements

* One host, loopback networking, local bootstrap and iroh relay. Real
  networks add RTTs and relay hops; the stale-peer cost (connect timeouts)
  would be the same or worse.
* B, D and the patched C are dev-profile builds (dependencies optimized,
  Holochain crates not); A and the stock C are release binaries.
* About 100 ops of one small entry type, one DNA, full or zero arcs only
  (Holochain 0.7.1 cannot configure partial arcs; partial and wrapping
  arcs are covered by Rust tests).
* "C" is a desktop process. Nothing here measures a phone's radio, battery
  or OS scheduling, or demonstrates background wake-up.
* Kitsune2 byte counts cover connections still open at measurement time.

## Milestone 2: three conductors, polling vs event-driven

Each row is one run of `scripts/holochain-three-node-e2e.sh`. The raw
`results.json` of each run is in [`results-data/`](results-data/); logs are
written to `.demo/three-node-*` (not committed).

Setup common to all runs:

* Three conductors on one host, local `kitsune2-bootstrap-srv` 0.5.3
  (bootstrap + iroh relay), full storage arc, `GOSSIP=fast`
  (`initiateIntervalMs 2000`, `minInitiateIntervalMs 1000`,
  `initiateJitterMs 500`).
* A and C: stock `holochain 0.7.1` release binary. B: stock 0.7.1 in polling
  mode; in event mode, holochain 0.7.1 **plus the U1 patch**, built locally
  with Holochain's dev profile (dependencies optimized, Holochain crates
  not), so B is somewhat slower than a release build.
* Relay on B: polling every 1000 ms (polling mode) or every 30 000 ms with
  the `OpsIntegrated` source (event mode, so latency comes from the event
  path and polling only reconciles).
* While C is offline, A authors 5 notes and B authors 1. An "observer"
  client stays online on SSE throughout.

Definitions:

* **Detection**: `observed_at_ms - occurred_at_ms` of each hint envelope,
  i.e. from B's integration of the op until the relay journaled it.
* **Notification**: from the relay journal to the online observer client's
  handler (`relay_to_app`); **end-to-end** adds detection (`origin_to_app`).
  All clocks are the same host's.
* **C hint sync**: C's device client catching up over HTTP while C's
  conductor is still stopped.
* **First op received / all integrated**: from C's own `DumpOpTimings`,
  relative to the moment C's conductor process was started.
* **Bytes**: HTTP bytes as seen by the client (request line, headers, body;
  header sizes estimated; TLS/TCP not included).

### Notification latency (relay on B)

| Run | Detection (B integrated → relay journal), ms p50 / max | Notification (journal → online client, SSE), ms p50 / max | End-to-end (B integrated → online client), ms p50 / max | Hint sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| polling, C bootstrap on, peer hint on | 199 / 247 | 11 / 19 | 210 / 266 | holochain-app-signals: 1, holochain-op-timings: 18 |
| polling, C bootstrap off, peer hint on | 237 / 365 | 9 / 16 | 246 / 381 | holochain-app-signals: 1, holochain-op-timings: 18 |
| event, C bootstrap off, peer hint on | 7 / 7 | 4 / 8 | 11 / 15 | holochain-ops-integrated: 22, holochain-app-signals: 1 |
| event, C bootstrap on, peer hint on | 4 / 6 | 1 / 7 | 5 / 13 | holochain-ops-integrated: 18, holochain-app-signals: 1 |

"Hint sources" counts the envelopes C received, by the relay source that
journaled them first. In event mode **every op hint came from the
`OpsIntegrated` path**, including B's self-authored ops. The poller
(30 s) contributed none before C woke up. On the same patched B, a second,
stock-crates relay (`holochain_client` 0.9.1, `on_signal` registered)
journaled the normal app signal and all 52 ops by polling, and never received
an `OpsIntegrated` signal (no decode panic in its log).

The relay's own counters (`/admin/v1/metrics`,

The data itself (the 18–22 ops) moved between conductors over Kitsune2
(iroh), not over HTTP, and is not included above.

### Reading the numbers

* **Event-driven detection is roughly 30–50× faster than 1 s polling.** p50 4–7 ms
  versus ~200–240 ms from B's integration to the relay journal; end-to-end
  to an online client p50 5–11 ms versus ~210–250 ms. Polling latency is
  bounded by the poll interval (≈ interval/2 on average), and a shorter
  interval costs a `DumpOpTimings` query per DNA per interval. B ran a
  dev-profile build in event mode, so the event numbers are, if anything,
  pessimistic.
* **SSE fan-out is cheap.** Journal to client handler is ~1–11 ms p50.
* **HTTP is not the bottleneck for catch-up; peer selection is.** C's
  HTTP sync took 5 ms and ~7.7 KB for 18 hints. After restart, C's
  conductor was ready in ~2.3 s.
  * With only the relay's peer hint (bootstrap off), the first missing op
    arrived ~5.2 s after start, and all were validated and integrated by
    ~6.2 s.
  * With bootstrap on, the peer store also held the offline A. Kitsune2
    chose A first (least recently gossiped) and waited out two connect
    timeouts, so catch-up took 47–69 s in these runs (up to 84 s in earlier
    exploratory runs). Once a round with B started, the
    ops arrived in ~25 ms and integrated within ~1 s.

  This is gap U2/U3 in `upstream-changes.md`.
* **The relay's peer hint is what made the no-bootstrap catch-up possible.**
  The control run, identical but without handing B's record to C, caught up
  on 0 of 18 ops in 150 s.
* **Missed notifications only delay delivery.** In the first event-mode attempt
  (patch v1, which only hooked the integration workflow), B's three
  self-authored ops produced no signal. That run failed the hint check
  because the 30 s poller had not reconciled them yet. With polling enabled
  they would have arrived on the next poll, and the regression test covers
  this explicitly. The final patch hooks the store instead.
* **Operation counts.** Six notes produced 15–17 new integrated ops on A
  and 18–20 on B. A's count is lower, most likely because some of B's note
  ops had not yet reached A when A's dump was taken. C received and validated all 18–22 hinted ops.
